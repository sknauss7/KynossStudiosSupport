The job is bigger than it looks

Board members are unpaid, change every year or two, and are personally on the hook for getting things right. Meanwhile the rules keep growing.

In Florida alone, recent laws added training for new directors, regular safety inspections and repair savings for taller condo buildings, and a requirement for larger associations to post their records online. Condo and HOA boards also follow different laws, so advice meant for one can be wrong for the other.

How it would work

Designed to fit around a volunteer's schedule, not take it over.

  1. It files what comes in

    Email, texts, portal documents and phone scans are read, filed and summarized overnight, so next year's board can find them.

  2. One message each morning

    What arrived, what's due soon and what needs a decision, on your phone.

  3. Every meeting starts with one page

    The decisions in front of the board, the options and a recommendation. Agendas and draft minutes too.

  4. It keeps the deadlines and the money

    Legal deadlines, inspections, insurance and contracts in one calendar. Budget against spending, and bids side by side.

  5. Open items close with one tap

    When a document shows something is done, it suggests closing it. Approve the list from your phone.

It knows when to call a lawyer

Sending every question to an attorney gets expensive, and sending none is risky. The assistant tells you which kind of question you have.

  • Your call

    Everyday business decisions and questions your documents answer clearly. It does the homework and makes a recommendation.

  • Worth asking

    Gray areas that haven't turned into a dispute. It writes up the question for your attorney and keeps it ready, so asking later is quick and cheap.

  • Call your attorney

    Lawsuits, demand letters, action against an owner, or anything you can't take back. It says so right away and hands over its notes, so the lawyer's time costs less.
