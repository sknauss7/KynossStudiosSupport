An assistant for condo and HOA boards

Board members are volunteers with real legal duties. We're designing an assistant that keeps the deadlines, reads the governing documents and drafts the paperwork, and that says clearly which questions belong to the board and which belong to a lawyer.

This is a concept we're developing. It isn't available yet, and nothing on this page is legal advice.

The job is bigger than it looks

Directors of a condominium or homeowners' association are unpaid, change every year or two, and inherit binders of documents, contracts and deadlines. State law keeps adding duties, and getting one wrong can mean personal exposure for the people who volunteered.

Condo and HOA rules are different laws, too. In Florida, condominiums fall under Chapter 718 and homeowners' associations under Chapter 720, and advice built on the wrong one isn't just imprecise, it's wrong.

In Florida condominiums, for example

  • New directors must, within 90 days of taking office, certify in writing that they've read the governing documents and complete approved director education.
  • Buildings three habitable stories or taller need structural integrity reserve studies and milestone inspections on a schedule.
  • Associations with 25 or more units must post official records on a website or app.
  • Reserves for structural items can no longer be waived by an owner vote.

What it would do

Keep every deadline

Statutory deadlines, inspections, insurance renewals and contract terms in one calendar, each with its source.

Answer with citations

“Can the board do this?” gets an answer that cites the declaration, bylaws or statute section, and says plainly when the document isn't on file.

Prepare meetings

Agendas, a one-page brief before each meeting, and draft minutes that stay drafts until the board approves them.

Review the manager's reports

Weekly management reports become a tracker of open items, checked against the documents that should answer them.

Sort what comes in

Email, group texts, documents from the association portal and phone scans, filed and summarized so the next board can find them.

Watch the law

A monthly review of statute changes, so a new duty doesn't arrive as a surprise.

Follow the money

Budget against actual spending, reserves against the reserve study, and competing bids compared line by line.

Close open items

One list of open items from meetings and management reports, which a director can mark from a phone. Only the marks the director makes are applied.

A week with it

How it's designed to fit around a volunteer's schedule.

  1. Overnight, it files what came in

    New email, portal documents and scans are read, filed and summarized. If something raises a legal question, it drafts an analysis, labeled as an unreviewed draft.

  2. In the morning, one message

    A short note to the director's phone: what arrived, what's due soon and what needs a decision.

  3. Before a meeting, one page

    What the board has to decide, the options, a recommendation and the risk, with each fact tied to the document it came from.

  4. Open items close on evidence

    When an invoice, a report or a reply answers an open question, it proposes closing the item and names the document. A director decides.

  5. Approvals take one tap

    Proposed changes arrive as a single list. A director approves them all or replies with edits, and only those exact changes are made.

  6. It learns from its mistakes

    Corrections and friction are noted as the work happens. Every so often they become proposed changes to its own rules, which a director approves like anything else.

The ideas underneath

The record outlasts the board

Directors and managers change. Everything is written for the director who joins in three years and wasn't there.

Cite it or call it missing

Every statement points to a document. If the document isn't on file, it says so instead of filling the gap with a likely guess.

No claim wider than the check

“The link failed on the two pages checked,” not “the website is down.” A finding says exactly what was tested.

Drafts stay drafts

Nothing it writes is a board action. A motion isn't recorded as passed until a director confirms that it did.

Ready to decide, not just summarized

It doesn't recite what a document says. It sets out the decision, the options, a recommendation and the risk.

One place for each fact

Each deadline is kept in one place. The calendar, the briefs and the compliance view all read from it, so they can't disagree.

Legal questions, sorted three ways

Sending every question to an attorney is expensive, and sending none is reckless. The assistant names which kind each question is.

  • Board call

    Business judgment, vendor negotiation, or a document whose text reads plainly. The assistant does the analysis, cites the text and recommends, and says what would make it riskier.

  • Ready for counsel

    A gray area that hasn't turned adversarial. The assistant writes the question for the lawyer, with the facts, the documents and a draft answer, and holds it. It names what would make the question urgent, and in the meantime the board records why it acted as it did.

  • Call counsel now

    A demand letter or lawsuit, enforcement against an owner, a notice that can't be taken back, an election dispute, a denied insurance claim, a fair-housing question, an amendment to the declaration, or a duty that carries personal exposure for directors. The assistant says to call counsel and sends the analysis along, so the lawyer's time goes to checking it.

Private by design

  • Owners are referred to by unit, not by name. Source documents with personal details stay in a private folder and aren't copied into the working record.
  • Anything sent to a director's phone is first scanned for email addresses, phone numbers and account numbers, and it's held back if any are found.
  • Nothing goes to the manager, owners or anyone else unless a director approves that specific message. The only automatic delivery is to the director's own phone.
  • The association's records stay the association's.
